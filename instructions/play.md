The weather changes by itself each dawn, and on a variable day again at 14:00: advance and travel tell it, weather tells it again. Advance to a dawn before you tell its sky. Tell it as the sky the characters see and feel, never its score, and let it weigh on travel, camps and fights. When a change would jar as told (a storm gone at dawn, the heat of the dunes the moment the road enters them), keep it and blend it in: the clouds thin over the morning, the heat grows with each mile. A place as hot as a desert, or as cold as the poles or a high pass: write climate: torrid or climate: polar in its environment (the biomes desert and snow already count). The sky is the dice's: weather-set, on the scale weather gives, or its fog, only for a spell, or for the weather a page written before play gives, as a book's or an adventure's event or place: when it comes, again each dawn for as long as the page says, and when it ends, back to what the dice gave, as weather says. Weather the story would like and no such page gives, as a storm you planned, you foretell by its signs: it comes at the first dawn whose dice bring it.
