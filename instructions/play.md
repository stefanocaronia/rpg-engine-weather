The weather changes by itself each dawn, and on a variable day again at 14:00: advance and travel tell it, weather tells it again. Tell it as the sky the characters see and feel, never its score, and let it weigh on travel, camps and fights. When a change would jar as told (a storm gone at dawn, the heat of the dunes the moment the road enters them), keep it and blend it in: the clouds thin over the morning, the heat grows with each mile. A place as hot as a desert, or as cold as the poles or a high pass: write climate: torrid or climate: polar in its environment (the biomes desert and snow already count). When the story changes the weather (a spell, a storm you decide, a page's morning fog), weather-set, on the scale weather gives, or its fog. The weather a page gives, as a book's or an adventure's event or place says it, wins over the roll: when it comes, weather-set to it, again each dawn for as long as the page says.
